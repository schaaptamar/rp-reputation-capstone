Reputation Management with AI Agents for Fertility Care

Eight students, four tracks, one system: collect what people say about an IVF clinic, understand it, check what AI assistants tell prospective patients, and alert the clinic when something changes.

Industry partner: Reproductive Partners Fertility Center - San Diego Mentors: MENTOR NAMES Team Websites: RPSD–UCSD Capstone SharePoint
Introduction

Two kinds of reputation

Choosing an IVF clinic is one of the most expensive and emotional decisions a patient makes, and prospective patients research it heavily. More and more of that research now happens by asking an AI assistant instead of reading reviews directly. That means a clinic has two reputations, and they can disagree.

Patient reputation

What real patients write in reviews, forums, and social media.

AI and search reputation

What Google, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude tell someone who asks about the clinic.

A clinic with mostly positive reviews can still be described poorly by an AI that leans on a few negative Reddit threads. A clinic with great care can go unmentioned because its public information is thin or outdated. This project builds the tools to see both, and to notice when either one shifts.

Research question: Can a set of AI-assisted tools continuously monitor an IVF clinic's patient and AI reputation, surface emerging issues early, and give staff a clear, trustworthy picture of what prospective patients encounter?

Two students per track

Project tracks

Each track has one guiding question, a set of methods to learn, a demo goal, and milestones. The goal is a working proof of concept that demonstrates the method, not a production system. A team that spends three weeks fighting a website's bot protection should switch to an API or a hand-collected sample and move on.

Track 1Team: STUDENT A, STUDENT B

Reputation data collection

What are people saying about the clinic, and where?

Scope

  • Pick two or three sources, such as the clinic's Google Business Profile, Yelp, Reddit, and fertility review sites.
  • For each source, decide the permitted method: official API, owner data export, or careful scraping where the terms allow it.
  • Normalize everything into reputation_item records, remove duplicates, and store them.
  • Run collection on a schedule and add only new items.

Track 2Team: STUDENT C, STUDENT D

Reputation analysis

What are people actually unhappy or happy about?

Scope

  • Define a topic list with the clinic: physician care, nursing, communication, billing and cost, scheduling and wait times, front desk, facilities, outcomes.
  • Score each review for overall sentiment, sentiment per topic, and severity.
  • Compare an LLM with structured output against a classical baseline.
  • Hand-label about 150 reviews and report accuracy on them.
  • Find emerging themes the topic list missed, using embeddings and clustering.

Methods to learn

sentiment analysisaspect-based sentimenttext classificationembeddingsclusteringLLM APIsJSON structured outputlabeling & inter-rater agreement

Milestones

Wk 3–4
Topic list and labeling guide; 150 labeled reviews.
Wk 5–6
Baseline and LLM classifiers; accuracy per topic.
Wk 7–8
Severity scoring and emerging-theme detection.

Demo: upload a CSV of reviews, click Analyze, and get a topic breakdown with representative quotes for each topic.

Track 3Team: STUDENT E, STUDENT F

AI reputation scanning

What is AI saying about Reproductive Partners Fertility Center - San Diego?

Scope

  • Write a fixed set of about 30 questions a prospective patient would ask. Mix branded ones ("Is Reproductive Partners a good clinic?"), comparison ones ("Reproductive Partners vs …"), and open local ones ("best IVF clinic in San Diego").
  • Run them against several assistants and search engines on a schedule, several times each, since answers vary from run to run.
  • Extract whether the clinic was mentioned, its position, sentiment, competitors, cited sources, and specific claims.
  • Check claims against a clinic-approved fact sheet to flag outdated or wrong information.

Wk 5–6

Demo: a report showing how often the clinic appears, how it's described, which competitors show up instead, which sources the AIs rely on, and any incorrect claims.

Track 4Team: STUDENT G, STUDENT H

Dashboard and alerts

What's changing, and what needs someone's attention today?

Scope

  • Interview clinic staff about what they'd check weekly and what they'd want to hear about right away.
  • Build a dashboard of rating trends, review volume, top topics and how they're moving, and AI visibility.
  • Write alert rules with thresholds, and test them on historical data so they don't fire too often.

Ground rules: privacy and ethics

Patients sometimes share very personal details in public reviews, such as failed cycles, diagnoses, or pregnancy loss. Being public doesn't make that information free to use however we like. Design privacy into every track from the start.

  • No data from clinic systems. No medical records, scheduling data, or patient lists. Work only with public content, synthetic data, and materials the clinic has cleared.
  • Never try to identify a reviewer or link a review to a real patient. Don't store reviewer names or profile handles.
  • Don't send review text to an LLM provider without checking how that provider stores and uses it. Use API tiers that don't train on your inputs.
  • No public replies. Responding to reviews is out of scope. Providers have been fined by HHS for disclosing patient details in replies to online reviews.
  • No fake reviews, paid reviews, or planted content to improve how AI describes the clinic. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake and paid reviews and review suppression.
  • Follow each platform's terms of service and API limits. If a site prohibits scraping, use another source.
  • Keep only the fields you need, and document how long data is kept and who can see it.
  • Report failures and limitations honestly, including where your classifiers or AI checks get things wrong.
